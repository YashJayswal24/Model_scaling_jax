\section{Three JAX Parallelism Modes}

The book identifies three contracts between programmer and JAX runtime for multi-device execution:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{L{2.8cm} L{3.5cm} L{4.5cm} L{2.8cm}}
  \toprule
  \rowcolor{tablehead}
  \thead{Mode} & \thead{API} & \thead{Who decides communication?} & \thead{Errors on ambiguity?} \\
  \midrule
  \roweven \textbf{Auto} & \codeinline{jax.jit} (Auto axes) & XLA / Shardy compiler & No --- may silently do wrong thing \\
  \textbf{Explicit} & \codeinline{jax.jit} (Explicit axes) & JAX type system & \textbf{Yes} --- errors on ambiguous sharding \\
  \roweven \textbf{Manual} & \codeinline{jax.shard\_map} & \textbf{You} --- fully explicit & N/A --- you write every byte \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

This notebook exercises all three modes, matching the book's pedagogical progression. Each mode's limitation motivates the next.

\section{Problem 1 --- Sharded Average \& Roll-Diff}

\textbf{Reference:} \S Worked Problems, Problem 1 \quad \textbf{Mesh:} $(x=2, y=4)$, AxisType.Explicit

\subsection{Per-Shard Average}

Array $A: \texttt{float32}[S_X, D_Y]$ sharded across the mesh. Goal: return $[X, Y]$ where each entry is the mean of shard $(i, j)$.

\subsubsection{With jax.jit (reshape trick)}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
@jax.jit
def average(arr):
    # Reshape [x, p, y, q] -> mean over local (non-sharded) dims (1, 3)
    arr = jnp.mean(arr, axis=(1, 3))
    return jax.lax.with_sharding_constraint(arr, P('x', 'y'))
\end{lstlisting}

\subsubsection{With shard\_map (device-local view)}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
@jax.shard_map(in_specs=jax.P('x','y'), out_specs=jax.P('x','y'))
def avg_shard_map(arr):
    assert arr.shape == (p, q)          # each device sees only its shard
    return jnp.mean(arr).reshape((1, 1))
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{Result:} \codeinline{[[4.5, 6.5, 8.5, 10.5], [20.5, 22.5, 24.5, 26.5]]} \checkmark --- zero communication on both.

\begin{calloutinfo}
\textbf{Key insight:} With \texttt{shard\_map}, the absence of any \texttt{psum} / \texttt{all\_gather} / \texttt{ppermute} in the function body \emph{proves} zero communication. This is impossible to verify with \texttt{jax.jit} without reading the compiled HLO.
\end{calloutinfo}

\subsection{Roll-Difference via shard\_map}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
@jax.shard_map(
    in_specs=(jax.P('x','y'), jax.P('x','y'), jax.P()),
    out_specs=jax.P('x','y')
)
def roll_diff(base, roll, shift):
    return base - jnp.roll(roll, shift=shift, axis=0)
# Result (shift=1): rows alternate between -8 and +8  checkmark
\end{lstlisting}

Profiler traces saved: \codeinline{/kaggle/working/shardmap\_avg/} and \codeinline{/kaggle/working/roll\_diff/}.

\section{Problem 2 --- Mixture of Experts Routing}

\textbf{Reference:} \S Worked Problems, Problem 2 \quad \textbf{Mesh:} $(x=8, y=1)$

\subsection{Dimensions}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{C{1.5cm} C{2cm} L{7cm}}
  \toprule
  \rowcolor{tablehead}
  \thead{Symbol} & \thead{Value} & \thead{Description} \\
  \midrule
  \roweven $E$ & 8 & Total experts (one per device) \\
  $S$ & 2048 & Total tokens ($S_x = 256$ per device) \\
  \roweven $D$ & 4096 & Hidden dimension \\
  $F$ & 14336 & Expert output dimension (Llama-8B FFN proxy) \\
  \roweven $N$ & 8 & Number of devices \\
  $C$ & 32 & Expert capacity --- tokens per communication round \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Naive jit MoE --- 73\,ms}

The natural scan-based approach iterates over all $E$ experts with a binary mask:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
def process_exp(carry, e):
    mask = (B == e)[:, None]   # [S, 1] -- which tokens belong to expert e
    to_add = A @ W[e]           # [S, F] -- ALL tokens x expert e's matrix
    return carry + to_add * mask, None   # E-1 results are thrown away

out, _ = jax.lax.scan(process_exp, output, jnp.arange(E))
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{What XLA did:} auto-sharding assigned \emph{both} $A$ and $W$ along axis $x$ (data-parallel). This forced an AllGather of all activations before the scan --- $O(S \times D)$ memory replicated per device.

\begin{calloutwarning}
\textbf{Observed latency: 73\,ms.} The AllGather alone dominates. At production scale ($S=10^6$, $D=8192$), this would replicate gigabytes of activations across every device on every forward pass.
\end{calloutwarning}

\subsection{Pipelined All-to-All --- 1.7\,ms \quad (43$\times$ faster)}

Instead of gathering all activations, use explicit \textbf{All-to-All} to route only the tokens belonging to each expert.

\subsubsection{Three-Phase Pipeline}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{L{2.5cm} L{3cm} X}
  \toprule
  \rowcolor{tablehead}
  \thead{Phase} & \thead{Name} & \thead{Description} \\
  \midrule
  \roweven \textbf{Phase 1} & PreFetch & Sort tokens by expert, bin into $[E, C, D]$ chunk, fire first All-to-All $\to [E_x, N{\cdot}C, D]$ \\
  \textbf{Phase 2} & Unrolled Pipeline & Python \texttt{for}-loop: compute current chunk while fetching next. XLA overlaps comm + compute in the flattened graph. \\
  \roweven \textbf{Phase 3} & Drain & Process last pre-fetched chunk, reverse All-to-All $\to [E, C, F]$, scatter back to $[S_x, F]$ \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\subsubsection{Key Communication Code}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
# Forward dispatch: send tokens to expert-owning devices
recv_A = jax.lax.all_to_all(
    send_A, axis_name='x', split_axis=0, concat_axis=1
)  # [E, C, D]  ->  [E_x, N*C, D]

# Dense local matmul -- zero wasted compute
recv_Out = jnp.einsum('edf,ecd->ecf', W_local, recv_A)

# Backward return: results back to origin devices
recv_Back = jax.lax.all_to_all(
    recv_Out, axis_name='x', split_axis=1, concat_axis=0
)  # [E_x, N*C, F]  ->  [E, C, F]

# Pipelined overlap (Python loop = unrolled in XLA graph)
for i in range(num_scan_steps):
    carry_out = compute_and_scatter(recv_A_curr, valid_curr,
                                    safe_idx_curr, carry_out)  # math first
    recv_A_curr, valid_curr, safe_idx_curr = fetch_chunk(i+1) # comm second
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{calloutsuccess}
\textbf{Observed latency: 1.7\,ms.} A \textbf{43$\times$ speedup} over the naive AllGather approach. AOT-compiled via \texttt{.lower(W, A, B).compile()} for clean, JIT-warmup-free profiling. Trace: \texttt{MoE\_chunk\_all\_to\_all/}.
\end{calloutsuccess}

\subsection{Top-$k$ MoE ($k=2$) --- Zero Overhead}

Extension to Top-2 routing (each token processed by 2 experts, averaged):

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
flat_B = B_local.reshape(-1)              # [S_x, 2] -> [S_x * 2]
flat_A = jnp.repeat(A_local, k, axis=0)  # duplicate each token k times
# ... same All-to-All pipeline ...
final_out = jnp.mean(final_out_k, axis=1)  # average k expert outputs
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{Observed latency: $\approx$1.7\,ms} --- identical to Top-1. The pipeline processes $2\times$ the tokens at the same capacity $C$ with the same number of communication rounds.

\section{Problem 3 --- Collective Matmuls \& Overlapped MLP}

\textbf{Reference:} \S Worked Problems, Problem 3 \quad \textbf{Dims:} $BX=D=4096,\ F=14336$ (Llama-8B proxy)

\subsection{Baseline --- jax.jit Auto-Sharded MLP (1.2\,ms)}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
# Mesh (x=4, y=2), AxisType.Auto
@jax.jit
def compute_mlp(In, Win, Wout):
    return jax.lax.with_sharding_constraint(In @ Win @ Wout, sharding)
# In -> P('x','y')   Win -> P(None,'y')   Wout -> P('y',None)
# Profiler: jit_MLP_Auto_Shard/ -- 1.2 ms total
#   AllReduce: ~0.2 ms  |  AllGather: ~0.1 ms
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{AllReduce Matmul via shard\_map}

Implements $A[BX,DY] \cdot W[DY,F] \to \text{Out}[BX,F]$:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
@jax.shard_map(
    in_specs=(P('x','y'), P('y',None)), out_specs=P('x',None)
)
def compute_matmul_allreduce(A, W):
    return jax.lax.psum(A @ W, axis_name='y')   # local matmul + AllReduce
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{ReduceScatter --- Three Variants}

For the down-projection $\text{Tmp}[BX,FY] \cdot W_2[FY,D] \to \text{Out}[BX,DY]$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{L{3.5cm} X C{2.5cm}}
  \toprule
  \rowcolor{tablehead}
  \thead{Variant} & \thead{Mechanism} & \thead{Latency} \\
  \midrule
  \roweven \textbf{psum\_scatter} & JAX built-in hardware-optimized primitive & \textbf{1.01\,ms} \checkmark \\
  Manual ring (unidirectional) & $y-1$ \texttt{ppermute} steps rightward; Python loop & 1.2\,ms \\
  \rowcolor{highlightrow} \textbf{Recursive halving} (bidirectional) & $\log_2(y)$ XOR-partner exchanges; array halves each step & \textbf{$\approx$1.1\,\textmu s} \checkmark \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The recursive halving pattern uses XOR to select partners --- a classic butterfly all-reduce:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
for step in reversed(range(int(math.log2(y)))):
    # XOR partner: device i exchanges with i XOR 2^step
    perm = tuple((j, j ^ (1 << step)) for j in range(y))
    recv_data = jax.lax.ppermute(send_data, axis_name='y', perm=perm)
    state = keep_data + recv_data   # array shrinks by 2x each step
# After log2(y) steps: [BX, D] -> [BX, DY], fully summed and placed
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{End-to-End Overlapped Transformer MLP (1.25\,ms)}

Composing AllGather up-projection + recursive halving ReduceScatter down-projection with GeLU:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
# Up-projection: AllGather along y to reconstruct full D dimension
In_gathered = jax.lax.all_gather(In_local, axis_name='y', tiled=True, axis=1)
act_local = jax.nn.gelu(In_gathered @ Win_local)   # [BX, FY_local]

# Down-projection: recursive halving ReduceScatter
final_out = compute_matmul_recursive_halving_rs(act_local, Wout_local)
# Out: [BX, DY] -- correct sharding at JIT-speed parity
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{callouttip}
\textbf{Why no speedup?} XLA already performs compute-communication overlap on TPU internally. Our manual overlap adds \emph{transparency} --- every byte is visible in Perfetto --- but not raw speed. The value is correctness guarantees and communication auditability.
\end{callouttip}
